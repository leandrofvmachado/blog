# Style Guide

> **Why this file exists:** the `draft` and `review` skills read this file to
> match Leandro's writing voice when generating or critiquing posts. It starts
> as a scaffold with open questions, not settled rules — nobody has supplied
> sample writing or preferences yet. Fill it in and revise it as more posts
> get written; treat it as living documentation, never a one-time form. If a
> section is still a question mark, the skills should fall back to the
> "(placeholder default, replace me)" note under it, if one is given, and
> otherwise stay neutral rather than invent a rule.

## Voice & Tone

Decide: informal or formal? First person ("I") or more detached? How much
humor/personality comes through? Who is the imagined reader — a peer
engineer, a beginner, future-you?

_(placeholder default, replace me): first person, conversational but
technical, light humor allowed, written for a fellow developer who's
comfortable with code but new to this specific topic._

## Sentence & Paragraph Habits

Decide: short punchy sentences or longer, winding ones? How long do
paragraphs typically run — one idea per paragraph, or several? Are rhetorical
questions used to transition between ideas?

_(placeholder default, replace me): mostly short-to-medium sentences,
paragraphs capped around 3-4 sentences, rhetorical questions used sparingly
to introduce a new section._

## Formatting Conventions

Decide: how deep do headings go (just `##`, or down to `####`)? When does
something become a code block vs inline code? How are links used — inline
mid-sentence, or collected at the end? Bullet lists vs prose for
explanations?

_(placeholder default, replace me): headings limited to `##`/`###`; inline
code for identifiers/commands, fenced blocks for anything runnable or
multi-line; links inline at first mention; prose preferred over bullets
except for enumerable steps or options._

## Words & Phrases to Avoid

Decide: any corporate jargon, hedge words, or AI-sounding filler phrases
(e.g. "it's important to note," "in today's fast-paced world," "leverage,"
"delve into") that should be flagged and cut on sight?

_(placeholder default, replace me): avoid "it's important to note that",
"in conclusion", "leverage" (use "use"), "delve into", "furthermore", and
unnecessary hedging like "it could be argued that"._
